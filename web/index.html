Know what to pick, what to remove and when to water.

A camera watches every orange and a few sensors watch the soil. Each morning the grower gets one message: which oranges are ready, which one has mold, and when to water next. The scanner below shows the camera part, using a colour check for now.

One state for every orange

State What the camera sees What you do
Unripe Mostly green peel Leave it on the tree.
Ripening Green turning yellow or orange Check again in a few days.
Ready to pick Even, deep orange peel Harvest it.
Possible mold White fuzz or blue-green patches, the signs of citrus green or blue mold Remove it before the mold spreads.

Watering, worked out for you

The daily message checks soil moisture first. When the soil is moist enough, the week’s estimated water demand sets the schedule.

Try the scanner

This version checks peel colour in your browser. It stands in for a small trained model (a CNN) until that model is ready, so treat its answer as a rough guide.

Frames never leave your device. Nothing is uploaded, and there is no backend.

How it’s built

So far

The scanner on this page. It converts each webcam frame to hue, saturation and value, then scores the peel colour, all in your browser.

Next

  • Train a small CNN on one of the team’s laptops, then run it locally on a laptop webcam to classify ripeness and spot mold.
  • Send the daily message: a Telegram bot posts the grower a summary at 9:00 every morning, and a /status command sends it on demand.
  • Advise on watering: from soil moisture, temperature, humidity, wind and the weather forecast, the message says how often to water and when the next watering is due. It is a rule of thumb, not an agronomist.
  • Swap the CNN in for the colour check on this page with TF.js or ONNX Runtime Web. The scanner keeps the classifier behind one interface, so the rest of the page stays as it is.

The sensor board

An Arduino board will read the grove’s sensors: soil, wind, temperature and humidity. The team is wiring them up now, so it sends no readings yet. Once they work, their readings feed the watering advice in the daily message. Later, the board will switch the irrigation and a picking arm.

Why oranges

We built this at Cutiehacks in Riverside, a city that grew up on oranges. The tree that started California’s navel orange industry still stands here.

Growers still walk the rows to judge ripeness, look for mold and decide when to water, one tree at a time. We wanted the grove to do the walking and send a short note instead.
